Castle Protect

The Unflattening · Native iOS, native Android, Web · 1 or 2 players · Free · Offline

A turn-based artillery game where your crew lives inside a walking fort you built yourself. Every shot breaks wood, stone and ground in a way you can read and plan. Win by bonking the whole enemy crew or popping the steam kettle at the heart of their fort.

Completely free: no price, no purchases, no ads, no timers, no energy. Two players can share one device. A hand-made campaign, a real tech tree, and an AI that misses like a person.

The world

Brickabrac, a lumpy land the Flatteners want rolled flat

Small, stubborn Hobs claim hills and build walking forts from whatever they can stack. Each fort runs on a Heartkettle that drives its clockwork legs. Baron Levelwell and his Flatteners want every hill rolled flat, because flat is tidy (and he once spilled his tea on a hill). You fight to keep Brickabrac lumpy, and every region you win back gets its hills back. The genre's worst habit, flat boring maps, becomes the story.

Tone: bloodless slapstick, safe for a nine-year-old, not babyish for a forty-year-old. A Hob at 0 HP is Bonked and floats off on a Snooze Balloon. Art style: "patchwork storybook", round chunky shapes and plum outlines. Hobs are round; Flatteners are square.

How a battle plays

Read it, then nail it

TurnsStrict turns. An optional Walk (costs Steam), then one Action. No turn timer.
AimDrag anywhere to aim. Lifting your finger does not fire; a big Fire button does. A Fine Tune pad and exact numbers stay on screen.
Aim helpThe preview shows 30% of the arc (45% and 60% with upgrades). Your last two shots stay as a Ghost with Pins. Recall restores an old aim once.
WindHolds for 3 rounds. The next front is shown in advance, free.
Two ways to winClean Sweep: bonk every enemy Hob. Kettle Pop: break their 300 HP Heartkettle. Both bars always show.
No stalematesAt round 15 the Sudden Squall starts: both forts crumble at once and wind grows. Every fight ends within 3 more rounds.
Read the fortHold any block to see what would fall. Weakly held blocks shimmer. A "Drop!" callout marks the first big collapse.
Loophole FlapAfter a shot threads a gun slot, that slot closes to threads until the wind changes. One kept aim cannot win every turn.
Shove OffDig out a buried Hob for 15 Steam during your Walk, not your whole turn.
Squall in 3A warning two rounds before the cap. Battles run about 4 to 6 minutes at real speed.

Fairness rules that answer the genre's top complaints

  • The AI misses like a person: wide first, then short, long, hit. It only sees what you see, and it has no hidden catch-up cheats.
  • No status effect ever skips your turn.
  • A "No line" mark warns before battle when a Hob has no clear shot on the map.
  • The game saves every turn. A crash or phone call never costs a match.
  • You never lose what you earned. If an update weakens something, you get a full refund.
  • The bottom row of every fort (Footing) cannot be dug, so "dig out the base" stops winning every time.
  • Your own blasts hurt you at 50% and never knock out your own Hob, and a buried fort always digs out. No unfair traps.

Before each battle you and the Rival draft 4 shells each from a pool of 10 (the Rummage). One formula scores every shot, and bots check 20 balance red flags each night.

Enemies and bosses

Fair bosses with tells, and a final fight about raising hills

  • Launch bosses: Marshal Trimm (Topiary Keep), Dame Scree (Sanding Stair), Admiral Drainwell (Great Sump), then Steward Plumb and Baron Levelwell (The Last Level).
  • Boss forts have 3 phases. A boss always warns a turn before a big attack and never heals back past a phase line.
  • The final boss has 2 stages, the Grand Roller then the Level Engine. You win by raising hills, and you can retry from the stage.
  • 14 enemy unit types at launch, 16 castle styles that each teach something, and 8 AI personalities such as Bully, Sniper, Gambler and Coward. Each Rival states one habit you can build against.

Progression

Two currencies, a free fort editor, no grind wall

ButtonsEarned in every battle, win or lose. Spent on blocks and Hob ranks.
PennantsUp to 3 per level. Spent on the Tinker Tree.
Tinker Tree4 branches (Aim, Fort, Crew, Kit), 36 nodes at launch, 60 in all. Free resets. Info readouts are free.
WorkshopBuild your fort with undo, mirror and test fire. 5 saved Blueprints. Blocks sell back at full price.

A script checked every level: a player who earns only 1 Pennant per level and never replays can still afford what each region needs. The launch campaign takes about 5 hours; full completion takes 12 to 14.

Two players

Couch Duel: two players, one device, at launch

  • Everything is open to both players, including the draft and hidden gadgets.
  • Even Starter Box forts are ready from the first launch, or bring your own fort and crew.
  • Help levels per player (None, Some, Lots) are shown openly.
  • An optional Kind Clock, off by default, nudges a slow player but never fires for them.
  • Win by knockout or by Volley Points. Table mode in portrait gives each player their own half.
  • No AI, fully offline, no rewards to farm.

Tech

Native apps on one shared rules core

Shared coreRust: rules, AI, content, saves, the Daily and bots. Whole-number maths (Q16.16 fixed point), so all three apps give exactly the same result
iPhone and iPadSwift, SwiftUI and Metal. The core ships as an XCFramework
AndroidKotlin, Jetpack Compose and OpenGL ES 3.0. The core ships as NDK libraries
WebTypeScript and WebGL2, with the core as WebAssembly. Installs and works offline
CollapseOur own support check in the core. Each renderer adds the creak, lean and dust
AIIts own thread, under 400 ms on a 2 GB phone
TestsThe same recorded battles must match on iOS, Android, Linux, macOS and three browser engines. 20,000 bot battles each night
MoneyNone. No purchase, ad or analytics code

Five options were scored. The shared Rust core won with 93 of 115. Three separate apps scored 69 and would cost about 290 engineer-weeks.
